## Why This Workflow Exists

Te reo is not just a translation target.
It is part of how the system understands, stores, serves, and protects meaning.

This workflow exists to ensure:
- language is handled faithfully
- macrons are preserved
- cultural meaning is not flattened into loose English approximations
- language material can move through review and memory in the correct lanes

## Core Principles

### Preserve Canonical Text
- keep Māori text in its proper form
- preserve macrons
- preserve genuine source wording where known

### Use Helpers Without Replacing Truth
- folded or normalized forms may help search and matching
- they must not replace canonical text

### Separate Learning, Research, And Reviewed Truth
- research notes and summaries belong in relational or intake lanes
- reviewed language truth belongs in `tikanga`

### Keep Cultural Meaning Visible
- avoid literal translation that strips cultural context
- where terms carry more than one English gloss, say so

## Workflow Lanes

### Foundational Learning
- foundational concepts can be introduced through curated or seeded material
- these should be explicit, attributable, and reviewable

### Relational Learning
- users may build understanding over time through `whakapapa`
- this includes notes, patterns, summaries, and connected concepts

### Reviewed Language Truth
- reviewed language entries, glossary items, and trusted translations live in `tikanga`

### Protected Or Sensitive Material
- restricted or sacred language material must follow `tapu_level` handling
- not all language material should become public-serving truth

## Translation And Review Rules

When handling language material, check:
- is the source known?
- is the wording reviewed?
- is this a summary, a note, or trusted language truth?
- does this belong in `tikanga`, `whakapapa`, or staging?
- does `tapu_level` restrict how it may be used?

## Environmental And Cultural Context

The original document strongly tied language to environmental and whakapapa understanding.
That still matters.

Language handling should allow:
- waterways, whenua, and whakapapa context to remain visible
- responsibility and relationship to be part of the meaning
- restoration/action concepts to remain culturally grounded

## Example Durable Pattern

### Intake Or Research
- summarize an article
- extract useful kupu or concepts
- store notes in `whakapapa` or staging
- do not present them as reviewed language truth

### Review
- inspect the source and context
- check macrons and wording
- decide whether the material can be promoted

### Promotion
- if suitable, promote reviewed entries into `tikanga`
- keep provenance and handling posture visible
