## Why Transparency Exists Here

Transparency exists to make the system accountable:
- financially
- operationally
- socially
- culturally

This matters because a guardian-based system asks people to trust not just code, but handling, intent, and boundaries.

## What Transparency Should Cover

### Funding
- where money comes from
- what kinds of support are accepted
- what conditions are attached, if any

### Allocation
- what the work funds
- how much goes to maintenance, contributors, operations, audits, reserves, or community support

### Governance
- who makes decisions
- who reviews or audits critical changes
- what conflict or accountability mechanisms exist

### Community Impact
- who benefits
- what kinds of use are supported
- whether free/community use is being protected in practice

### Integrity
- whether implementation choices are being driven by mission or by compliance theater, extraction, or vendor lock-in

## Transparency Principles

The system should be able to explain:
- what it is doing
- why it is doing it
- who benefits
- what is protected
- what tradeoffs were made

Transparency should not require sacrificing:
- sensitive data
- protected cultural material
- private user records
- restricted operational details that would weaken safety

## What A Good Transparency Report Includes

- funding categories
- budget allocation categories
- contributor/community support
- major risks and lessons
- priorities for the next period
- how claims can be verified

These can be adapted for yearly, quarterly, or milestone-based reporting.

## Current Translation

The original transparency report was a detailed annual accountability report for one older product and funding model. The durable meaning is still strong:
- transparency builds trust
- money and decision-making should not be hidden
- community benefit should be visible

This rewrite keeps those values while making the document applicable across future implementations and governance structures.
