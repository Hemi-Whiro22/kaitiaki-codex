## Why This Exists

This policy exists to make a boundary between:
- community or public-benefit use
- extractive or large-scale commercial use

The durable intention is simple:
- free for people and communities
- not free for corporate free-riding

## Durable Eligibility Principles

The following kinds of use align with the free/community intent:
- individual learning or personal work
- educational and research use
- public-interest or community-benefit use
- grassroots, cooperative, or volunteer-run use
- public institutions acting for public benefit

The following kinds of use usually require a different arrangement:
- large-scale commercial use
- reselling the system as a service
- proprietary deployment that avoids reciprocal obligations
- extractive use that benefits capital while giving little back

## What Should Be Judged

Eligibility should be judged by:
- mission and public benefit
- governance and accountability
- scale and commercial posture
- whether the project is being used extractively

Not just by:
- one arbitrary number in isolation
- whether an organization can technically pay

## Declarations And Review

A lightweight declaration process can still be useful.

The durable rules are:
- make eligibility expectations clear
- let people self-identify their use case
- leave room for review, hardship, and exceptions
- prefer clarity and good faith over bureaucracy

## Relationship To Ownership And Transparency

This document should align with:
- `OWNERSHIP.md`

That prevents contradictions between:
- who the project is meant to serve
- how it is sustained

## Current Translation

The original document provided a detailed threshold-based eligibility policy. The lasting meaning is stronger:
- the system should stay free for genuine public/community use
- the project should not become free labor for corporate extraction
- policy should be visible, fair, and humane

This rewrite keeps that policy role while avoiding one fixed set of dated numeric thresholds pretending to be timeless.
